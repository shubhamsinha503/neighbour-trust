Prominence–Interpretation (Fogg)

Trust cues have to be seen to work

The source strip and confidence tags moved out of a footer legend into the main flow, right under the score — a cue nobody notices does no credibility work, however good the data behind it is.

Pratfall effect (Aronson)

The honesty banner is deliberate, not a disclaimer

A source that visibly admits what it doesn't know reads as more trustworthy than one that's uniformly confident — but only once basic competence is established, which is why it sits below the verdict, not above it.

Loss aversion (Kahneman & Tversky)

One clear watch-out beats six equal scores

People weigh a flagged risk more heavily than an equivalent gain. Surfacing the single biggest concern up top — instead of burying it in a grid of six same-size cards — is what a buyer will actually remember and act on.

Contagious (Berger) — STEPPS

The share card is the growth loop

Home buying in India is a family decision. A card designed to look good in a WhatsApp preview — practical value plus social currency for whoever sends it — is what turns one buyer into the next five leads.

Social proof (Cialdini)

Numbers, not adjectives

"184 verified residents" and "2.3K buyers viewed this month" do more trust-building work than any amount of marketing copy — especially for exactly the categories (safety, water, power) where official data is thinnest.
